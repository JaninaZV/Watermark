Direct cooling water is what the data center itself consumes — evaporative cooling towers, chilled-water loops, humidifiers. Direct cooling water is now region-specific from vendor reports; see sidebar for per-region citations.

Indirect water is freshwater consumed upstream at the power plants that supplied the electricity. This dwarfs direct water and varies wildly by grid mix: hydropower evaporates reservoirs (~17 L/kWh on average), nuclear and fossil need cooling water (1–3 L/kWh), wind and solar PV are near zero. This is why Sweden — the cleanest carbon grid in the comparison — uses the most water: its grid is heavily hydropowered, so even with near-zero direct cooling water the indirect generation water dominates.

Watershed stress — the same litre of water means more in a high-stress basin than in a low-stress one. We surface this with a stress weighting derived from WRI Aqueduct baseline water stress (modest multiplier: gross × (1 + stress score)). Basin-level data may not match a specific facility's intake.
